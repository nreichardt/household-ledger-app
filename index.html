Household Ledger

A private finance and health-record app that one family runs for itself, on its own computer.

What it is

Household Ledger gathers one family's own financial records — bank and card accounts, loans, bills, pay statements, receipts — into a single ledger, so the household can see what it owns, what it owes, and where its money goes. It runs on a computer in that family's home. There is no company behind it, no service to sign up for, and no version of it that anybody else uses.

Why it connects to health providers and insurers

Medical care is one of the household's larger expenses, and the most difficult to see clearly. A card statement shows a payment to a hospital; it does not show what the payment was for, what the insurer allowed, what is still owed, or that a monthly charge is repaying a balance rather than buying care. Reading a member's own claims and balances — with that member's permission, through the standards-based APIs their insurer and provider publish — is what turns those charges into something a family can plan around.

The app asks only for the records of the people in the household, and only after each of them authorizes it at their own provider or insurer.

Where the data goes

  • Onto one computer in the household's home, and nowhere else.
  • Not to us, because there is no us — no servers, no accounts, no operator.
  • Never sold, never shared, never used for advertising, never used to train a model.

The privacy policy sets this out in full, including how to withdraw access and what happens to the data when you do.

Who it is for

One family. It is published here because connecting to a health provider's or an insurer's API requires an application to have a public home page, privacy policy and terms of service that a member can read before authorizing anything. That is the only reason this page exists.
