Privacy policy

Household Ledger · last updated 13 September 2026

Household Ledger is an application one family runs for itself on a computer in its own home. It has no operator, no server, no customer accounts and no business model. This policy describes what the application does with the information it is given, in plain language, and it is short because the application does very little with it.

What information the application handles

  • Financial records the household connects or imports: accounts, balances, transactions, loans, bills, pay statements and receipts.
  • Health information the household authorizes: insurance claims and explanations of benefits, and clinical records held by the household's own healthcare providers.
  • Amounts a provider says are owed, and the schedule of any payment plan.

It handles the records of the members of one household only, and only for those members who have authorized it themselves.

How that information may be accessed, exchanged or used — and by whom

It is used for exactly one purpose: to show the household its own financial and medical position on its own screens.

  • It is never sold. Not now, and there is no future in which it is — there is no company to sell it and no party to sell it to.
  • It is never shared with any third party, for any purpose, including advertising, analytics, research or marketing.
  • It is never used to train any model. No opt-in is offered, because none is wanted.
  • It is not disclosed to anyone outside the household except where the law requires it.
  • The only people who can read it are the adults in the household, signing in to the application on their own home network.
  • It is sent back only to where it came from, and only on request. Today the application only reads. If a future version can send information to a member's own healthcare provider where that provider's API allows it, it will send only what that member chose to send, to that provider alone, after the member has authorized it separately — and this policy will say so before it does.

Express consent, before anything is accessed

The application cannot reach a provider or an insurer on its own. Each member authorizes it directly at their own provider or insurer, using their own login there, and that authorization is what issues the application its access. Nothing is retrieved before that consent is given.

Because the information is never shared or sold, there is no separate consent for sharing or selling to give — those things do not happen. If that ever changed, it would require fresh express consent from the member first, and this policy would say so before it happened.

Other information on your device

The application accesses no other information from a member's phone or computer. It reads no contacts, no location, no photos, no messages, no other applications and no device identifiers. It is a web application the household opens in a browser on its own network.

It does read a dedicated email mailbox the household set up for it, which receives only forwarded order confirmations, bill notices and one-time security codes. The household controls what is forwarded into it and can stop that at any time.

Where the information is kept, and how it is protected

  • Stored in the United States, on a computer in the household's home. It is not stored in any cloud service operated by anyone else.
  • Encrypted in transit. All connections use HTTPS, and the application refuses to establish a session over an unencrypted connection.
  • Encrypted at rest. Credentials and second-factor secrets are individually sealed with AES-256-GCM under a key held outside the database and outside the directory containing it. The database file itself is protected by whole-disk encryption on the machine that holds it.
  • Backups are encrypted with a passphrase distinct from the application's own key.
  • The application is built following current web application security practice, including the OWASP Top 10, and its developers have reviewed the HL7 FHIR Security and Privacy Module.
  • Every read of health information is recorded in an audit log the household can inspect.

Withdrawing access, and what happens to the data

You can stop the application at any time, in either of two ways, and they do different things:

  • Revoke at the source. Withdraw the application's authorization at your provider's or insurer's own portal. Its access ends immediately and it can retrieve nothing further. It never holds your provider or insurer password, so there is nothing else for it to use.
  • Disconnect inside the application. This deletes the stored authorization and stops all further retrieval.

Disconnecting deliberately leaves the records already retrieved in place, so that changing insurer does not silently erase the household's own history. To dispose of that data as well, delete it in the application, which purges the records and anything derived from them on a documented schedule, after a short recovery window that exists so an accidental deletion can be undone. The household can also export everything it holds, as JSON or CSV, at any time.

Children

Two children's records may be reached, only through a parent's own authorized access as that child's personal representative, and only for as long as the provider permits it. Their records are handled exactly as this policy describes.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the updated date above changes with it. A change that widened how information may be used would require fresh consent before it applied to anything already held.

Contact

This application is operated by the household that uses it. Questions about it can be sent to the address on the developer registration held by the provider or insurer through which you authorized it.
