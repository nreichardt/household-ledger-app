What this application is

Household Ledger is a personal, non-commercial application that one family runs on its own computer to keep track of its own money and its own medical records. It is not a product, it is not offered to the public, and it is not available for anyone else to sign up to or install. These terms exist because connecting to a healthcare provider's or an insurer's API requires a published set of them.

Who may use it

The adults of the one household that operates it, signing in on their own home network. There is no registration, because there is nobody else to register.

What it does not do

  • It gives no financial advice. Nothing it displays is a recommendation to buy, sell, borrow, invest or refinance. Its forecasts and valuations are estimates produced from the household's own records, they are labelled as estimates, and they can be wrong.
  • It gives no medical advice. It displays claims, balances and records that the household's providers and insurers produced. It does not interpret them, and it is not a substitute for talking to a clinician.
  • It moves no money. It cannot pay a bill, transfer funds, trade, or change anything at a bank or an insurer. Every financial connection it holds is read-only, and that is enforced in the software rather than promised: a financial connector that could write cannot be registered.
  • It changes nothing at a provider without being asked to. Today every health connection is read-only as well. If a future version sends information back to a member's own healthcare provider — a completed questionnaire, say — where that provider's API permits it, it will do so only for the member who asked, only for what they asked to send, and only after they have authorized that separately at the provider. It will never alter the clinical record of its own accord.
  • It is not a medical record. The authoritative copy of any clinical record remains with the provider that holds it.

Accuracy

The application reproduces what banks, insurers and providers report to it. Those sources are sometimes late, incomplete or wrong, and where the application derives a figure of its own it says so. Nobody warrants that any number shown is correct, and the household is responsible for checking anything that matters against the statement it came from.
